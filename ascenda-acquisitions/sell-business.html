What we look for

Business profile

  • Steady earnings, generally $500K a year or more
  • Owners ready to retire, step back or exit
  • Repeat customers; no single customer a large share of revenue
  • Simple operations with a manager or a team that can run it
  • Services, distribution, light manufacturing, healthcare support, property services, logistics
  • Metro Atlanta and the Hudson Valley, including Rockland and Orange County

What we generally avoid

  • Businesses dependent on one customer or one key employee
  • Early-stage or loss-making companies
  • Heavy regulation we cannot responsibly take on
  • Highly speculative or hard-to-verify financials

Structure

Ways a deal can be built

Cash at closing

The simplest structure when the numbers and diligence line up.

Seller note

A portion of the price paid over time, often 10% to 20%, which also aligns interests during the transition.

Transition period

You stay on, paid, for an agreed period to introduce customers and train the team.

Bank or SBA financing

Where appropriate, deals can be paired with SBA 7(a) or 504 financing.

Timeline

From first note to closing

Typical small-business deals take roughly two to four months, depending on records and third-party approvals.

01

Confidential first note

Industry, location, revenue and earnings. No names needed yet.

02

NDA and financials

We sign a mutual NDA, then review three years of financials and tax returns.

03

Letter of intent

Price, structure, timing, exclusivity and what we need to verify.

04

Confirmatory diligence

Financials, customers, contracts, leases, employees, licenses and legal items, reviewed alongside your advisors.

05

Definitive agreement and close

Attorneys draft the purchase agreement. Funds move at closing and the transition begins.

Prepare

What helps a sale go smoothly

  • Three years of profit-and-loss statements and tax returns
  • Year-to-date financials and a current balance sheet
  • List of top customers and the share of revenue each represents
  • Equipment, leases and key contracts
  • Org chart and roles of key employees
  • Why you are selling and the timeline you prefer

FAQ

Questions owners ask

Will you tell my employees or customers?

Not before you are ready. Confidentiality comes first, and we agree together who is told, when and how.

Will the business name and team stay?

In most cases yes. We buy to keep operating what you built.

Do I need a broker?

No, but you are welcome to bring one. We pay attention to broker relationships and welcome referrals.

Is this an investment opportunity?

No. We buy businesses for our own account. We are not offering or soliciting any securities.
